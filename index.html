About

Connecting digital work and human decisions

Liwei Chen is an Associate Professor in the Department of Operations, Business Analytics, and Information Systems at the Carl H. Lindner College of Business, University of Cincinnati.

Her research examines how the design and use of digital technologies shape human interaction and influence problem-solving and decision-making in both societal and business contexts. This includes work on digital collaboration, data-driven decision support, and the integration of emerging technologies such as AI into complex organizational settings.

Liwei’s work has appeared in leading journals including MIS Quarterly, Information Systems Research, Journal of the Association for Information Systems, European Journal of Information Systems, and Journal of Medical Internet Research, and has been presented at conferences such as the International Conference on Information Systems, INFORMS, the Academy of Management Annual Meeting, and the Annual Workshop on Health IT and Economics. She currently serves as an Associate Editor at MIS Quarterly.

Lab 4 · Investment analysis workflow

Lab 4 — Investment Analysis Workflow

An agentic workflow in Dify that turns raw market data into structured, AI-powered investment insights for any stock.

This lab guides students to build a multi-step agentic workflow in Dify that connects to Yahoo Finance, retrieves live stock data, and orchestrates AI agents to interpret key indicators such as price history, volatility, and valuation ratios.

The workflow generates a concise, explainable summary of a stock’s recent performance, risk profile, and long‑term outlook, helping students think critically about how AI and data can support—but not replace—human investment judgment.

Lab 5 · Event impact analyzer

Lab 5 — Portfolio Event Impact Analyzer

A multi-step AI workflow that traces how global events ripple through a stock portfolio and suggests BUY/SELL/HOLD actions.

In this lab, students design an AI pipeline that takes a news event or scenario (for example, a policy change, supply shock, or earnings surprise) and analyzes how it might affect holdings across a portfolio.

The workflow combines event understanding, sector and firm-level mapping, and impact reasoning to produce structured BUY/SELL/HOLD recommendations. Students must explicitly encode uncertainty, assumptions, and conditions under which a recommendation might change.

Dify Yahoo Finance Scenario analysis
Workflow view
Events → Decisions
1. Parse event
Use AI to extract affected sectors, regions, and risk drivers from an event description.
2. Map to holdings
Link event drivers to specific portfolio positions using fundamentals and exposure data.
3. Recommend actions
Generate BUY/SELL/HOLD suggestions with rationales and highlight where human review is critical.
Lab 6 · Gemini CLI portfolio analysis

Lab 6 — Gemini CLI Portfolio Analysis

A command-line workflow that uses Google Gemini to analyze portfolio data and surface investment recommendations directly in the terminal.

This lab brings AI-assisted analysis into a lightweight developer workflow. Students write shell commands that load portfolio data, call the Gemini CLI, and prompt the model to identify concentration risk, diversification opportunities, and holdings that may need closer attention.

By working in the terminal, students learn to think of AI not only as a UI feature but as a programmable tool that can be composed with scripts, schedulers, and other automation to support ongoing investment monitoring.
